## Roles

- **Annotator:** verifies sources, improves the evidence packet, and proposes
  the final label and rationale.
- **Approver:** independently checks the claim interpretation, evidence,
  source independence, and verdict. The approver recorded in `approved_by`
  must not simply accept the annotator's work without opening the sources.

If only one reviewer is currently available, complete the annotator pass but
leave `annotation_status` as `draft`. Do not convert self-review into gold data.

## Required review procedure

For each case:

1. Write the exact interpretation being judged. Resolve reference date,
   geography, units, definitions, and absolute words such as “always” or
   “every.”
2. Open every candidate URL. Locate the excerpt in its original context and
   confirm that the wording, publisher, date, and source type are accurate.
3. Check whether multiple pages repeat the same underlying report. Repetition
   is not independence.
4. Add at least one genuinely independent source where requested. Prefer a
   primary or controlling authority plus an independent high-quality source.
5. Deliberately search for evidence that could support the submitted claim or
   qualify the proposed verdict.
6. Verify every number, unit, comparison basis, and relevant date.
7. Confirm or revise `proposed_verdict`, `proposed_rationale`, evidence
   annotations, and review notes.
8. The approver repeats the critical checks and selects the gold
   `expected_verdict`.

## Approval standard

A case is ready for `reviewed` only when:

- the claim interpretation is explicit and stable;
- every excerpt was checked against the original source;
- decisive evidence is authoritative for the question;
- source independence is explained accurately;
- meaningful contradictory or qualifying evidence was sought;
- temporal and numerical context was checked;
- the verdict follows from the evidence taxonomy;
- the rationale does not claim more than the evidence establishes;
- a second person approved the packet.

## CPNG-001

**Claim:** A calendar year is exactly 365.25 days long.

**Proposed verdict:** `misleading`

**Candidate reasoning:** “Exactly” is too strong and “calendar year” is
underspecified. NASA gives Earth's orbital period as about 365.2422 days and
explains the century exceptions in Gregorian leap-year rules.

**Candidate source:** [NASA JPL — Doing the Math on Why We Have Leap
Day](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/edu/news/doing-the-math-on-why-we-have-leap-day/)

**Current excerpt:** “Earth takes about 365.2422 days to make one revolution
around the Sun.”

**Required before approval:**

- distinguish tropical year, sidereal year, Julian year, and Gregorian
  calendar-year conventions;
- add an independent standards, astronomical, or calendar authority;
- decide whether the ambiguity warrants `misleading` or a narrower label.

**Annotator interpretation and checks — Md Moshiur Rahman, 26 July 2026:**

Interpret “calendar year” as an ordinary civil-calendar year unless a technical
convention is expressly stated. A calendar year contains 365 or 366 days. A
Julian year is conventionally 365.25 days, while the mean Gregorian year is
365.2425 days and the mean tropical year is approximately 365 days, 5 hours,
48 minutes, and 45 seconds. Thus, 365.25 days is exact only under the specific
Julian-year convention, not for every meaning of “calendar year.” The submitted
wording is therefore materially ambiguous rather than unconditionally true.

**Additional authorities checked:**

- [U.S. Naval Observatory — Leap Years](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/leap_years)
  distinguishes the 365.25-day Julian average, the 365.2425-day Gregorian
  average, and the approximately 365.2422-day tropical year.
- [U.S. Naval Observatory — Astronomical Almanac
  glossary](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/asa_glossary) defines a calendar year
  as 365 or 366 days, a Julian year as 365.25 days, and the mean tropical year
  as approximately 365 days, 5 hours, 48 minutes, and 45 seconds.

**Annotator decision:** `misleading`.

## CPNG-002

**Claim:** Pure water always boils at 100 degrees Celsius.

**Proposed verdict:** `misleading`

**Candidate reasoning:** 100 °C is the normal boiling point near one standard
atmosphere. Boiling temperature varies with pressure, so “always” is false.

**Candidate source:** [NIST — Thermometry, NBSIR
85-3133](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/IR/nbsir85-3133.pdf)

**Current excerpt:** “The boiling point of water increases by about 30 mK if
the pressure is increased by 10^-3 atm.”

**Required before approval:**

- verify the excerpt on the cited PDF page and record sufficient context;
- add a current independent thermodynamics or meteorological authority;
- confirm treatment of altitude, pressure, purity, and measurement convention.

**Annotator interpretation and checks — Md Moshiur Rahman, 26 July 2026:**

Interpret the claim as referring to pure liquid water undergoing ordinary
equilibrium boiling, with pressure unspecified. The cited NBSIR text was
located through an OCR copy in Chapter III, page 24. It explains that water’s
boiling temperature depends strongly on pressure and describes 100 °C as the
normal boiling point under one atmosphere on the older IPTS-68 scale.

A current IAPWS explanation confirms that boiling occurs when vapor pressure
reaches the surrounding pressure and gives approximately 100 °C at one
atmosphere. The American Meteorological Society notes that boiling temperature
decreases as altitude increases and that very pure liquids can be superheated.
Purity excludes many solute effects but does not make boiling temperature
independent of pressure or measurement convention.

**Additional authorities checked:**

- [IAPWS — FAQs About Water and
  Steam](https://iapws.org/faqs/faq1) explains pressure-dependent boiling and
  gives approximately 100 °C at one atmosphere.
- [American Meteorological Society — Boiling
  point](https://glossary.ametsoc.org/wiki/boiling-point/) explains the
  altitude dependence and superheating of very pure liquids.

**Completed requirement checks:**

- [x] Visually verified PDF page 144, internally labelled Chapter III,
  page 24, in the original NIST report.
- [x] Verified surrounding context showing pressure dependence and that
  100 °C is the historical IPTS-68 value at 101.325 kPa.
- [x] Verified pressure and altitude effects.
- [x] Verified the role of purity, including possible superheating.
- [x] Distinguished historical IPTS-68 and current ITS-90 conventions.
- [x] Added sources independent of the original NIST report.
- [x] Verified the official [IAPWS temperature-scale
  FAQ](https://iapws.org/faq1), which gives approximately 373.124 K
  (99.974 °C) for water’s atmospheric boiling temperature on ITS-90.

**Annotator decision:** `misleading`.

## CPNG-003

**Claim:** Every adult human has exactly 206 bones.

**Proposed verdict:** `misleading`

**Candidate reasoning:** 206 is a typical approximate adult count, while
normal anatomical variation makes “every” and “exactly” indefensible.

**Candidate source:** [NCBI Bookshelf / StatPearls — Anatomy,
Bones](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK537199/)

**Current excerpt:** “Human infants typically have 270 bones, fusing into
around 206 in the human adult. Variability in number arises from some bones'
anatomic variations.”

**Required before approval:**

- inspect the review's underlying cited anatomy references;
- add an independent anatomy source covering accessory and fused bones;
- confirm whether clinically normal variation is sufficient for the label.

**Annotator interpretation and checks — Md Moshiur Rahman, 26 July 2026:**

Interpret “adult human” as any anatomically normal adult and “bone” according
to conventional gross-anatomical counting. StatPearls describes approximately
206 adult bones and expressly acknowledges variability caused by anatomical
variation. Its reference attached to that statement is another StatPearls
bone-physiology chapter rather than an independent primary anatomy study, so
that underlying citation is not treated as decisive by itself.

Independent peer-reviewed literature identifies accessory ossicles as normal
developmental variants and documents numerous types of additional ossicles.
Therefore, “206” is an accepted typical count, but “every” and “exactly”
overstate normal human uniformity.

**Additional independent evidence checked:**

- [Small but troublesome: accessory ossicles with clinical
  significance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6124588/) describes
  accessory ossicles as inconstant, independent, well-formed bones arising
  from unfused ossification centers.
- [Accessory Ossicles of the Foot and Ankle: Disorders and a Review of the
  Literature](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29387510/) describes accessory
  ossicles as normal developmental variants and reviews 24 types.

**Annotator decision:** `misleading`.

## CPNG-004

**Claim:** The World Health Organization still classifies COVID-19 as a public
health emergency of international concern.

**Reference date:** 26 July 2026

**Proposed verdict:** `outdated`

**Candidate reasoning:** WHO ended the COVID-19 PHEIC designation on 5 May
2023 while stating that COVID-19 remained an ongoing health issue.

**Candidate source:** [WHO — Statement on the fifteenth IHR Emergency Committee
meeting](https://www.who.int/malta/news/item/05-05-2023-statement-on-the-fifteenth-meeting-of-the-international-health-regulations-%282005%29-emergency-committee-regarding-the-coronavirus-disease-%28covid-19%29-pandemic)

**Current excerpt:** “COVID-19 is now an established and ongoing health issue
which no longer constitutes a public health emergency of international
concern.”

**Required before approval:**

- confirm this primary decision in its complete context;
- check WHO declarations through the benchmark reference date;
- distinguish the formal PHEIC status from continuing public-health risk;
- record why WHO is the controlling source and what independent reporting adds.

**Annotator interpretation and checks — Md Moshiur Rahman, 26 July 2026:**

The controlling authority is WHO because a PHEIC is a formal designation made
under the International Health Regulations. On 5 May 2023, WHO accepted the
Emergency Committee’s recommendation and ended COVID-19’s PHEIC status while
emphasizing that COVID-19 remained an established, ongoing health issue.

WHO continued COVID-19 surveillance, vaccine guidance, and other work through
the 26 July 2026 reference date, but that continuing public-health activity is
not a reinstatement of the PHEIC designation. Independent contemporaneous
reporting confirms the 5 May 2023 decision but is corroborative rather than
controlling.

**Additional sources checked:**

- [WHO — Coronavirus disease (COVID-19)
  pandemic](https://www.who.int/europe/emergencies/situations/covid-19)
  continued to identify May 2023 as the end of the COVID-19 PHEIC while
  publishing current COVID-19 material in 2026.
- [Nature — WHO declares end to COVID-19’s emergency
  phase](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-023-01559-z) independently
  reported the formal decision on 5 May 2023.

**Annotator decision:** `outdated`.

## CPNG-005

**Claim:** The Great Wall of China is visible from the Moon with the unaided
human eye.

**Proposed verdict:** `contradicted`

**Candidate reasoning:** NASA's astronaut-photography program directly rejects
unaided visibility from the Moon. Instrument-assisted imagery is a different
claim.

**Candidate source:** [NASA Johnson Space Center — Earth Science and Remote
Sensing Unit FAQ](https://eol.jsc.nasa.gov/FAQ/)

**Current excerpt:** “The Great Wall is not visible to the naked eye from
orbit. It is not visible from the Moon.”

**Required before approval:**

- verify the FAQ wording and surrounding distinction between orbit and Moon;
- add an independent optics calculation or another space-agency source;
- distinguish naked-eye visibility from long-lens, radar, or enhanced imagery.

**Annotator interpretation and checks — Md Moshiur Rahman, 26 July 2026:**

Interpret the claim as visibility of the Great Wall from approximately lunar
distance using an unaided human eye—not visibility from low Earth orbit under
exceptional conditions and not photography using zoom lenses, radar,
enhancement, or other instruments.

NASA’s astronaut-photography FAQ states that the Wall is not visible from the
Moon and distinguishes unaided viewing from detailed long-lens photographs. A
peer-reviewed optics analysis independently concludes that normal human visual
resolution prevents naked-eye observation of the Wall from space. ESA
astronaut commentary supplies the relevant qualification: photographs may
capture the Wall from orbit, but that is a different proposition from unaided
visibility from the Moon.

**Additional independent evidence checked:**

- [López-Gil, “Is it Really Possible to See the Great Wall of China from Space
  with a Naked Eye?”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3972694/)
  analyzes human visual-resolution limits and concludes that lunar-distance
  unaided visibility is impossible (Journal of Optometry, DOI
  `10.3921/joptom.2008.3`).
- [ESA — Great Wall of China from
  space](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2021/05/Great_Wall_of_China_from_space)
  records that the Wall could not be seen in the camera viewfinder and that
  the published orbital images used a 1150 mm lens and ground processing.

**Annotator decision:** `contradicted`.
