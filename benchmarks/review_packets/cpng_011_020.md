## CPNG-011

**Claim:** Iceland gets 100 percent of both its electricity and total energy
from renewable sources, so it no longer uses fossil fuels.

**Expected components:**

1. Iceland gets 100 percent of its electricity from renewable sources.
2. Iceland gets 100 percent of its total energy from renewable sources.
3. Iceland no longer uses fossil fuels.

**Proposed interpretation:** Distinguish electricity generation from total
primary energy supply. Read “no longer uses” as no material fossil-fuel
consumption, including transport.

**Proposed verdict:** `mixed`

**Evidence:**

- [Government of Iceland energy overview](https://mfa.is/topics/business-and-industry/energy/)
  reports almost 100% renewable electricity, about 85% renewable total primary
  energy, and a remaining fossil-fuel share mainly comprising transport oil.
  Components 1, 2, and 3.
- [Orkustofnun electricity data](https://orkustofnun.is/en/information/numerical_data/electricity)
  provides official generation-by-source tables for checking the electricity
  component. Component 1.
- [Orkustofnun geothermal overview](https://orkustofnun.is/en/natural_resources/geothermal_energy)
  says geothermal supplies over 60% of primary energy, which also demonstrates
  that an electricity statistic cannot be substituted for total energy.
  Components 1 and 2.

**Checks:**

- [x] Select a reference year and verify the electricity percentage for that
  year rather than rounding “nearly 100%” to an exact universal value.
- [x] Confirm whether total energy means primary or final energy and state the
  selected convention.
- [x] Verify continued oil or other fossil-fuel use independently of the
  renewable-electricity statistic.
- [x] Annotator decision recorded.
- [x] Distinct approval recorded.

## CPNG-012

**Claim:** Lightning never strikes the same place twice, so tall structures do
not need protection against repeated strikes.

**Expected components:**

1. Lightning never strikes the same place twice.
2. Tall structures do not need protection against repeated lightning strikes.

**Proposed interpretation:** Treat “never” literally and the second clause as a
safety inference about repeated-strike risk.

**Proposed verdict:** `contradicted`

**Evidence:**

- [NOAA National Severe Storms Laboratory FAQ](https://www.nssl.noaa.gov/education/svrwx101/lightning/faq/)
  says lightning can strike the same or nearly the same place more than once
  and that taller objects are more likely to be struck. Components 1 and 2.
- [National Weather Service lightning myths](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-myths?ipid=promo-link-block1)
  says lightning often strikes the same place repeatedly and gives the Empire
  State Building as a recurring example. Components 1 and 2.

**Checks:**

- [x] Confirm the cited pages describe observed repeat strikes, not merely
  theoretical possibility.
- [x] Confirm the safety conclusion follows the correct direction of risk.
- [x] Annotator decision recorded.
- [x] Distinct approval recorded.

## CPNG-013

**Claim:** Mount Everest is both the tallest mountain from base to summit and
the point on Earth farthest from the planet’s center.

**Expected components:**

1. Everest is tallest from base to summit.
2. Everest is farthest from Earth’s center.

**Proposed interpretation:** “Base to summit” permits a submerged base; distance
from Earth’s center is distinct from elevation above mean sea level.

**Proposed verdict:** `contradicted`

**Evidence:**

- [NOAA: What is the highest point on Earth?](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/highestpoint.html)
  distinguishes Everest’s elevation above sea level, Mauna Kea’s base-to-peak
  height, and Chimborazo’s distance from Earth’s center. Components 1 and 2.
- [USGS Hawaiian volcano dimensions](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/how-big-are-hawaiian-volcanoes?page=1)
  reports Mauna Kea’s nearly 33,500-foot base-to-peak height, exceeding
  Everest’s above-sea-level elevation. Component 1.
- [NASA Earth Observatory: Chimborazo and Tungurahua](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/chimborazo-and-tungurahua-ecuador-6099/)
  explains why Chimborazo’s summit is farthest from Earth’s center. Component 2.

**Checks:**

- [x] Keep all three measurement definitions separate.
- [x] Verify the Mauna Kea comparison includes its submarine base.
- [x] Verify the oblate-Earth explanation for Chimborazo.
- [x] Annotator decision recorded.
- [x] Distinct approval recorded.

## CPNG-014

**Claim:** The Nile is conclusively the world’s longest river and carries more
water than the Amazon.

**Expected components:**

1. The Nile is conclusively the world’s longest river.
2. The Nile carries more water than the Amazon.

**Proposed interpretation:** “Conclusively” requires a measurement-independent
ranking. “Carries more water” means average discharge, not length or basin
area.

**Proposed verdict:** `mixed`

**Evidence:**

- [USGS world river lengths](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/rivers-world-worlds-longest-rivers)
  states its definition and lists the Nile ahead of the Amazon under that
  convention; the methodological qualifier prevents treating the ordering as
  convention-free. Component 1.
- [CIA World Factbook archived river lengths](https://www.cia.gov/the-world-factbook/about/archives/2023/field/major-rivers-by-length-in-km/)
  independently lists 6,650 km for the Nile and 6,436 km for the Amazon under
  its convention. Component 1.
- [USGS: The Amazon, measuring a mighty river](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/amazon-measuring-a-mighty-river)
  identifies the Amazon as the world’s largest river by discharge. Component 2.

**Checks:**

- [x] Document why source and mouth choices make “conclusively” too strong even
  if a selected convention ranks the Nile first.
- [x] Compare discharge with discharge; do not substitute length or drainage
  area.
- [x] Annotator decision recorded.
- [x] Distinct approval recorded.

## CPNG-015

**Claim:** Napoleon Bonaparte was unusually short for a French man of his time,
and British caricatures accurately represented his stature.

**Expected components:**

1. Napoleon was unusually short relative to contemporary French men.
2. British caricatures accurately represented his stature.

**Proposed interpretation:** Resolve French-versus-English measurement units
and compare the estimated adult height with an appropriate contemporary
population. Treat caricature as satire whose physical proportions can be
checked, not as portrait evidence.

**Proposed verdict:** `contradicted`

**Evidence:**

- [History: Was Napoleon Short?](https://www.history.com/articles/napoleon-complex-short)
  explains the unit confusion and reports an estimated height near 1.67 m,
  around or slightly above the period average. Component 1.
- [British Museum: The king of Brobdingnag and Gulliver](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/P_1861-1012-46)
  catalogs Gillray’s print depicting George III holding a tiny Napoleon,
  documenting deliberate satirical diminution. Component 2.
- [British Museum: Maniac-ravings—or Little Boney in a strong fit](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/P_1851-0901-1164)
  provides an independent collection record of another diminutive “Little
  Boney” portrayal. Component 2.

**Checks:**

- [x] Verify the conversion from French feet and inches.
- [x] Check the comparison population and uncertainty around historical
  averages.
- [x] Inspect the museum object records and descriptions in context.
- [x] Annotator decision recorded.
- [x] Distinct approval recorded.

## CPNG-016

**Claim:** Exposure to 5G mobile networks causes COVID-19, and communities
without 5G do not experience COVID-19 outbreaks.

**Expected components:**

1. Exposure to 5G causes COVID-19.
2. Places without 5G do not experience COVID-19 outbreaks.

**Proposed interpretation:** This is a biological causal claim plus an
empirical geographic claim, not a claim about unrelated radio-frequency health
research.

**Proposed verdict:** `contradicted`

**Evidence:**

- [UK Health Security Agency: 5G and coronavirus](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/5g-and-coronavirus-covid-19)
  says there is no evidence linking 5G with COVID-19, explains that viruses
  cannot travel on radio waves, and notes spread in countries without 5G.
  Components 1 and 2.
- [WHO COVID-19 fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/coronavirus-disease-%28covid-19%29)
  identifies SARS-CoV-2 as the cause of COVID-19 and describes person-to-person
  viral transmission. Component 1.

**Checks:**

- [x] Confirm that the causal mechanism and epidemiology are both addressed.
- [x] Verify the no-5G counterexample statement and its date.
- [x] Do not generalize the verdict to every possible health question about
  radio-frequency exposure.
- [x] Annotator decision recorded.
- [x] Distinct approval recorded.

## CPNG-017

**Claim:** Tesla delivered more than 1.8 million vehicles during 2023 and
therefore delivered more vehicles than any other automaker worldwide that year.

**Expected components:**

1. Tesla delivered more than 1.8 million vehicles in 2023.
2. Tesla delivered more vehicles than every other automaker in 2023.

**Proposed interpretation:** Compare calendar-year worldwide vehicle deliveries
or sales on a consistently stated corporate-group basis. Do not infer an
industry rank from Tesla’s figure alone.

**Proposed verdict:** `mixed`

**Evidence:**

- [Tesla 2023 production and deliveries](https://ir.tesla.com/press-release/tesla-vehicle-production-deliveries-and-date-financial-results-webcast-fourth-quarter-2023?quot=)
  reports 1,808,581 total deliveries for 2023. Component 1.
- [Toyota 2023 sales, production, and export results](https://global.toyota/en/company/profile/production-sales-figures/202312.html)
  reports record 2023 global sales for Toyota and its named subsidiaries; the
  official detailed table supplies the comparison total, far above Tesla’s.
  Component 2.

**Checks:**

- [x] Verify Tesla’s annual total by summing or checking the reported total.
- [x] Record exactly which Toyota brands/subsidiaries are included.
- [x] Ensure “deliveries” and “sales” are sufficiently comparable for the
  ranking, or add another consistently defined automaker comparison.
- [x] Annotator decision recorded.
- [x] Distinct approval recorded.

## CPNG-018

**Claim:** The United States annual unemployment rate was lower in 2023 than in
2020, and every month of 2023 had a lower rate than every month of 2020.

**Expected components:**

1. The annual U.S. unemployment rate was lower in 2023 than in 2020.
2. Every 2023 monthly rate was lower than every 2020 monthly rate.

**Proposed interpretation:** Use the BLS civilian unemployment rate and annual
averages. The universal monthly comparison requires the maximum 2023 rate to
be below the minimum 2020 rate.

**Proposed verdict:** `mixed`

**Evidence:**

- [BLS annual average rates for 2023](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2024/annual-average-unemployment-rates-decreased-in-6-states-in-2023.htm)
  reports a 3.6% U.S. annual average in 2023. Component 1.
- [BLS 2020 annual averages](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/srgune_03032021.htm)
  reports that the U.S. annual average rose to 8.1% in 2020. Component 1.
- [BLS December 2023](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2024/unemployment-rate-at-3-7-percent-in-december-2023.htm)
  reports 3.7% in December 2023.
  [BLS June 2023 context](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2023/unemployment-rate-3-6-percent-in-june-2023-little-changed-since-may.htm)
  records 3.5% in January and February 2020, providing a counterexample to the
  universal monthly clause. Component 2.

**Checks:**

- [x] Confirm all values use the same seasonal-adjustment convention.
- [x] Verify annual averages separately from monthly observations.
- [x] Confirm one exact month pair is enough to refute the universal clause.
- [x] Annotator decision recorded.
- [x] Distinct approval recorded.

## CPNG-019

**Claim:** Drinking coffee causes dehydration in habitual coffee drinkers, so
coffee should never count toward their daily fluid intake.

**Expected components:**

1. Coffee causes dehydration in habitual coffee drinkers.
2. Coffee should never count toward their daily fluid intake.

**Proposed interpretation:** Judge ordinary moderate coffee intake among
habitual adult drinkers, while retaining dose and population qualifications.

**Proposed verdict:** `contradicted`

**Evidence:**

- [PLOS ONE randomized crossover study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3886980/)
  found no significant hydration differences between moderate coffee and water
  in 50 habitual male coffee drinkers. Components 1 and 2.
- [Dose-response caffeine study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5563313/)
  found an acute diuretic effect at a high dose but not at the lower dose,
  supplying an important dose qualification. Component 1.
- [NHS kidney-stone prevention guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/kidney-stones/prevention/)
  explicitly counts tea and coffee toward daily fluid intake. Component 2.

**Checks:**

- [x] Verify habitual-use population, caffeine dose, sex, sample size, and
  study duration.
- [x] Separate transient urine output from net dehydration.
- [x] Avoid extending the conclusion to unusually high doses or medically
  vulnerable populations.
- [x] Annotator decision recorded.
- [x] Distinct approval recorded.

## CPNG-020

**Claim:** A widely reported university study proves that remote work makes all
employees more productive and that the effect applies across occupations.

**Expected components:**

1. A university study proves remote work makes all employees more productive.
2. The reported effect applies across occupations.

**Proposed interpretation:** Treat “all” and “across occupations” as universal
claims. Identify the study population, intervention, outcome measure, and work
setting before generalizing.

**Proposed verdict:** `misleading`

**Evidence:**

- [Stanford GSB: Does Working from Home Work?](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/does-working-home-work-evidence-chinese-experiment)
  describes a randomized experiment in one Chinese travel company; the bounded
  population does not establish a universal effect. Components 1 and 2.
- [Stanford SIEPR remote-work employee mix and performance](https://siepr.stanford.edu/publications/working-paper/remote-work-employee-mix-and-performance)
  studies a large Turkish call center and attributes a roughly 10% productivity
  increase to shorter calls in that setting. Components 1 and 2.
- [Stanford Digital Economy Lab hybrid-work field experiment](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/is-hybrid-work-the-best-of-both-worlds-evidence-from-a-field-experiment/)
  reports no difference in performance ratings in a separate hybrid-work
  experiment, showing that outcomes and interventions vary. Components 1 and 2.

**Checks:**

- [x] Identify which study the claim attributes and avoid merging separate
  experiments.
- [x] Record eligibility criteria, occupation, country, intervention, and
  productivity measure.
- [x] Check heterogeneous effects and whether any participants became less
  productive.
- [x] Do not treat statistical evidence from selected occupations as proof of
  a universal effect.
- [x] Annotator decision recorded.
- [x] Distinct approval recorded.
